The parts

What the system does

Two air springs hold up the rear of the car. They replace the steel coil springs. The front axle keeps its steel springs.

A height sensor on each side measures the distance between the body and the axle. The control unit compares the height with a target. If the body is too low, the control unit starts the compressor and opens a valve. Air goes into the spring and the body goes up. If the body is too high, the control unit releases air.

When the height is correct, all valves are closed. The air stays in the springs. A good system holds its height for many days with the engine off.

What happens on the road

During driving the valves are closed. No air goes in or out of the springs. But the pressure changes all the time, because the volume of each spring changes.

  • Bump: the wheel goes up, the spring gets shorter, and the same air has less space. The pressure goes up and pushes the wheel down again.
  • Pothole: the wheel goes down, the spring gets longer, and the pressure goes down. Then the wheel hits the far edge of the hole and the pressure goes up sharply.
  • One wheel: the spring on the other side changes only a little. The body turns a small quantity around its length axis.
  • Speed bump: the two springs get shorter at the same time. The body goes up and comes down again. The shock absorbers stop this movement after one or two cycles.

Add load and do the test again. The pressure in the springs is higher, and the pressure changes are larger.

Three tests in the model

  1. Move the load to 400 kg. The body goes down. Then the compressor lifts it to the target.
  2. Select Left spring leaks. Look at the compressor. It starts again and again. Then tap Park for 8 hours and start the engine.
  3. Select Left sensor is wrong. The car leans, but there is no leak. The control unit thinks that the height is correct.

From symptom to part

One side is low after a night. It goes up after you start the engine.The air spring on that side leaks, or its air line connection leaks.
Both sides are low after a night.Two old springs, or a leak at the valve block.
The rear stays low. You do not hear the compressor.Fuse, relay, compressor motor, or wiring.
The compressor runs for a long time. The car goes up slowly or not at all.A weak compressor, or a large leak.
You hear the compressor frequently while you drive.A small leak. Repair it before the compressor wears out.
The car leans, but it holds the same position for days.A height sensor or its link rod.

Before you work on the car

  • Do not open an air line when there is pressure in the system. Release the pressure with a BMW diagnostic tool first.
  • Put the body on stands. Do not let the air springs hold the car while you work below it.
  • Find leaks with water and soap. Bubbles show the leak.
  • Repair the leak before you replace a compressor. A leak makes a new compressor wear out quickly.
  • When you replace the compressor, replace the relay also.
